## Topic Pitch: Waiting or Paying? 
### Provisional Project Title: Waiting or Paying? Is It Getting Harder to Get Healthcare in Ireland?
Topic: Why some people in Ireland do not get the medical care they need, because of waiting or because of cost, and how this has changed since 2016 compared with the EU.

### Why It Matters
Almost 693,000 people were on the public hospital outpatient waiting list in September 2026 [1]. In a Eurostat survey, 2.1% of people in Ireland said in 2025 that they went without a medical examination because of the waiting list, up from 1.0% in 2016. Over the same years the share who said cost was the reason fell from 1.5% to 0.6% [2]. So waiting now seems to stop more people than cost does, but it is not clear how big this change is, whether it is only an Irish pattern, or whether it follows the waiting list.

### Possible Questions
-	Has the main reason people in Ireland go without medical care moved from cost to waiting, between 2016 and 2025?
-	Is the pattern in Ireland different from the EU average?
-	Do the number of people on the hospital waiting list and the share who say waiting stopped them getting care go up and down together?

### Possible Data Source(s)
Eurostat - Open Data Browser:
-	Unmet need for medical care by reason and age group, 2016 - 2025 Unmet need for medical care by reason (too expensive or waiting list), all ages, Ireland and the EU average, 2016 - 2025 [2].
National Treatment Purchase Fund (NTPF) - Open Data: 
-	Monthly waiting list counts by specialty and time band, with age group (0-15, 16-64, 65+) up to March 2021 Monthly national outpatient waiting list, by how long people have waited, January 2019 - September 2026 [1].

### Expected Stakeholder(s)
-	Department of Health and HSE, patient advocacy groups, health researchers, journalists, and anyone in Ireland trying to get timely care.

Why Visualisation May Be Useful
The evidence is a yearly survey percentage and a monthly hospital count. In tables it is hard to see when waiting became a bigger reason than cost, whether Ireland differs from the EU, and whether the waiting list moves with what people report. Line charts that put the two sources side by side make this easier to see.
