# Team Charter and Task Allocation Plan

*Waiting or Paying? Is It Getting Harder to Get Healthcare in Ireland?*

## 1. How the team works

Each member has one primary responsibility and reviews another member's work, so every part of the project is checked by a second person. Responsibilities may differ, but all members are expected to understand the complete project and be able to answer questions on any major decision in the final presentation.

## 3. Project understanding

- At each weekly meeting, one member (rotating) explains a decision outside their own area and the team corrects it if needed.
- Before the presentation, each member answers questions on another member's area (data sources, chart choices, AI suggestions, etc.).
- Every member reads each submission fully before it is submitted on Moodle.

## 5. Communication, files, and decisions

### Communication

- WhatsApp group for quick questions and reminders
- Our shared team board for tasks, owners, reviewers, deadlines and status
- ~30 minute weekly meeting (in person/online), plus extra meetings before each submission. Everyone should reply to a direct request within 24 hours.

### File sharing

- One shared OneDrive folder with sub-folders used for almost the whole project (documents, raw & prepared data, figures, AI logs). Files are named topic_version_date (e.g. `unmet_need_by_reason_v1`).
- Code is stored on a private GitHub repository and shared with the team.

### Review process

- Each document has one editor at a time, who notifies the group on WhatsApp when finished.
- The secondary reviewer then checks it against the original source or brief.
- Everyone does a final check against the submission requirements (including IEEE references and no predetermined conclusion).

### Decision documentation

A shared Decision Log in OneDrive records each major decision (data cleaning, aggregation, chart types, etc.) with the date, the decision, alternatives considered, the reason, and who agreed. Used to support the oral defence.

### Resolving disagreements

1. Discuss it at the next meeting, with each person giving their evidence (the data, the brief or a visualisation principle).
2. If still unresolved, test it, for example by drawing both chart versions or checking the source.
3. If still unresolved, the majority decides and the minority view is noted in the Decision Log.
4. In a question about the brief, we ask the lecturer.

## 6. How AI use is recorded

Jenny maintains the AI Decision Log, but every member records their own AI use. Within 24 hours of using AI for anything that influences the project, the member adds a row:

| Stage | AI contribution | Decision | Explanation | Who / date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| e.g. Cleaning | What AI suggested | Accepted / Rejected / Modified | Why, with evidence from our data | Name, date |

- **Check:** every AI suggestion about data, charts, wording or claims is checked by a second member against the source or brief before it is used.
- **Selection:** we submit about 5-10 significant cases (accepted, rejected or modified) and keep their prompts and outputs in the AI log folder.
- **Human judgement:** we note decisions that needed human judgement, such as defining the audience, judging unusual values and whether a conclusion is justified, as evidence for the individual reflections.
